The maths, and what it leaves out

Swap

With fee f and γ = 1 − f:

out = x · γ · Rout / (Rin + x · γ)

The whole input joins the reserve, so k never falls.

Optimal size

A route of pools composes into a → Aa / (B + Ca). With a flash fee φ and λ = 1 + φ:

a* = (√(AB/λ) − B) / C
profit* = (√A − √(λB))2 / C

Gas is fixed, so it only shifts profit down.

Who is paid

With no fee, the arbitrageur's profit equals the LP's loss-versus-rebalancing:

E[LVR] = V0(1 − e−σ²T/8)

Gas paid by arbitrageurs goes to validators and is part of the LP's loss, not the arbitrageur's gain.

Assumptions and limits

  • Illustrative pools. Reserves and prices are inputs (default: two 10,000 ETH / 20,000,000 USDC pools at $2,000). Nothing is fetched live. USDC and DAI are treated as $1.
  • Costs. Net profit = swap output − input − flash-loan fee − gas. Gas is gas used × gas price × ETH price, and every attempt pays it.
  • Not modelled. Competition from other searchers (priority fees and MEV auctions usually take much of the profit), other trades in the same block, price moves before inclusion, and Uniswap v3 ticks when sizing arbitrage (v3 appears only as an impermanent-loss range).
  • LP simulation. The external price is geometric Brownian motion; arbitrageurs move the pool to the edge of the fee band after every step; uninformed volume is price-neutral and only pays the fee.
  • Cycles. Sizing uses each pool once per loop; trades execute against the pools shown, one at a time.
